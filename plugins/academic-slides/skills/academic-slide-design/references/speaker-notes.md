# Speaker notes

Write a full spoken script, not reminders. Under pressure a speaker reads what is there; a complete script in the speaker's own voice keeps the talk on time and on message.

## Format

Each main-body slide has a notes block with three parts:

- **Purpose**: what the slide must achieve, with the planned minutes, for example "(about 1 min)".
- **Script**: the sentences to say, in first person, in quotation marks, and in the language of delivery. Explain what the braces and colors mean the first time they appear.
- **Cue** (optional): what to point at; when to pause; how to answer the most likely question; which backup slide to open.

Other heading sets work too (for example 目的／講／操作), as long as the deck uses one set throughout.

## Timing

- The planned minutes across main-body slides should add up to the slot minus a buffer, for example 45 minutes minus 2.
- Record the per-section minutes in the project README, and rehearse once with a timer.
- Decide in advance which slides to shorten if time runs over, usually overview or secondary-evidence slides. Never cut the main theorems.

## What moves into the notes

- Limitations and scope statements removed from the slide.
- The reasoning behind an equality the slide shows without comment.
- Answers to anticipated questions, for example about a constant in a bound, or why one method wins on one metric and loses on another.
- The transition question at the end of a section, when it would look odd as slide text.
