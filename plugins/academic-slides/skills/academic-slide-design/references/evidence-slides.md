# Simulation, empirical, and overview slides

## Simulation studies

- **Place each study after the theory it supports.** A precision study follows the precision theory; a robustness study follows the robustness theory.
- **No letter tags (A, B, C) and no colored badges.** Title by study type and finding, for example "Precision study result: …" and "Robustness study setting: …".
- **Setting slide(s).** State the design (sample sizes, grid, replications, number of trajectories) and restate the methods compared, each with its defining criterion and citation. If the methods table and the design table do not fit side by side, use two setting slides.
- **Result slide.** One table or figure, highlighting the numbers you discuss, with one line on why they matter.
- **Teaser simulation** (one that only motivates the problem): a result slide only, with its setting on a backup slide.
- **Metrics.** Name them on the setting slide ("coverage, width, coverage at every t"); put their definitions, with formulas, on a backup slide. Put on another backup slide the numbers a careful listener may ask about, such as a robustness variant or the main effects of design factors.
- **Numbers.** Take them from the final version of the source. If the dissertation was rerun before submission, use the submitted tables, not earlier defense slides.

## Empirical studies

- Put the model on the same slide as the figure. Without it, the audience cannot tell what the figure estimates.
- Give a data note with source, years, and sample size, in a small table or one line.
- If the source has no estimating equation (for example a descriptive event study), use a short design table: event, comparison group, estimate, inference.
- Do not quote numbers from a version that is still changing; say "see the latest version" in the notes.

## Research overview in a job talk

- Use one slide per study, plus one map slide.
- Pick the dynamic or descriptive results that best support the map. Move secondary figures to the backup rather than squeezing two studies onto one slide.
- Keep economic or substantive detail short. The question for the audience is what can be identified or guaranteed, not the policy story.
